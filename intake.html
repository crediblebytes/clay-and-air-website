1. Parties and scope

2. Description of services

I understand that Practitioner offers some or all of the following, and that one session may combine several of them:

  • Breathwork. Slow, relaxing breathing; Dynamic Breathwork, which is rapid breathing with breath holds; and Sonic Neural Breathwork, which is sustained, connected breathing done lying down with the eyes covered, to music, for about fifty five minutes, with the express intention of bringing on a non-ordinary state of consciousness. Breathwork may include movement, shaking, vocalizing and emotional release.
  • Sound. Recorded music played through a sound system, tuning forks and handpan.
  • Ceremony. Setting intentions, burning resins such as frankincense, myrrh and dragon's blood, an open fire, and a share circle.
  • Cold water immersion. An ice plunge, when the session is held at Practitioner's home.
  • Supportive touch, only as I consent to it in this form.

No controlled, psychoactive or plant substances. Practitioner does not give, supply, source, recommend, prepare or supervise the use of any controlled substance, psychedelic, plant medicine or other psychoactive substance in connection with the Services. Any non-ordinary state referred to in this Agreement comes from breath, sound, movement and attention alone. If I use any such substance before or during a session, I do so entirely on my own, against Practitioner's instruction, and I may be removed from the session without a refund.

No medical, psychological or other licensed services. See Section 3.

5. Acknowledgment of risks

I understand that taking part carries inherent and foreseeable risks, as well as risks that cannot be fully anticipated or eliminated, and that these risks can result in minor injury, serious injury, permanent disability, psychological harm or death. I have read the risks below and I accept them.

Physical practice. Muscle strain, sprains, and joint, back or neck injury; dizziness, fainting and nausea; overheating and dehydration; cardiovascular events, including raised blood pressure, arrhythmia, cardiac event or stroke; aggravation of any existing condition, injury or surgical repair; injury from fatigue, distraction or going past my own limits; and injury from equipment, flooring, other participants or the venue.

Breathwork. Sustained fast breathing changes the level of carbon dioxide in the blood and can have marked physical and psychological effects. These include hyperventilation and hypocapnia; dizziness, lightheadedness and headache; tetany and carpopedal spasm, which is involuntary cramping and clawing of the hands, feet, arms and face that can be intense and can last for a while after the breathing slows; tingling, numbness and temperature changes in the hands and feet; fainting, loss of consciousness and injury from falling; nausea and vomiting; significant changes in heart rate and blood pressure; seizure in people prone to them; chest pain and difficulty breathing; increased pressure in the head, eyes and chest; risk to a pregnancy, including to the baby; strong emotional release, including uncontrollable crying, shaking, screaming, rage, grief, terror and involuntary movement; the sudden return of traumatic, repressed or forgotten memories, which may be distressing and may stay distressing after the session ends; non-ordinary states of consciousness, including changes in perception, vision, hearing and sense of time, depersonalization, derealization and ego dissolution; psychological destabilization, including disorientation, panic, prolonged anxiety, a depressive episode, insomnia, dissociation, intrusive images, mania or a psychotic episode, during the session, in the hours or days after, or later; and the possibility that memories or impressions arising in a non-ordinary state feel vivid and true but are not historically accurate.

Ceremony, sound and fire. Emotional and psychological material surfacing; disorientation and an altered sense of time; distressing imagery; reactions to sustained loud music or sound, including headache, nausea or, in people prone to them, seizure; reactions to resin smoke, including breathing irritation and asthma flare ups; burns from an open fire; spiritual or existential distress; and conflict between the practice and my own religious, cultural or personal beliefs.

Cold water. Cold shock, including gasping, rapid breathing and panic; sudden changes in heart rate and blood pressure, including arrhythmia; numbness and loss of grip and coordination; hypothermia; slips and falls on wet or icy surfaces; and fainting in or near water.

The setting and other people. Risks from the actions, inaction, negligence or conduct of other participants, assistants, space holders, volunteers, guests or third parties; risks from the condition of the premises, venue, parking, grounds, stairs, flooring, temperature, ventilation or equipment; risks of travel to and from a session; and risks of exposure to communicable illness.

7. Right to stop and self-responsibility

I understand that I am the final authority on my own body and my own experience. I agree that:

  • I will practice within my own limits and will not push past pain, dizziness, breathlessness or distress.
  • I will modify, rest or stop at any point, without needing permission or a reason.
  • I will not attempt any technique or practice I have reason to believe is unsafe for me.
  • I will tell Practitioner if I become unwell, distressed or overwhelmed. Practitioner cannot know what I am experiencing inside unless I say so, and I accept responsibility for saying so.

8. Integration and aftercare

I understand that what comes up in these practices may keep affecting me after the session ends, sometimes for days or longer. I understand that:

  • Practitioner may recommend rest, water, not driving for a while after breathwork, and not making major decisions right afterwards, and I agree to follow those recommendations.
  • Practitioner does not provide clinical integration, therapy or crisis support.
  • If I have significant or lasting distress, I will contact a licensed mental health professional, my doctor, emergency services, or the 988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline by calling or texting 988 in the US.
  • I have been advised to have my own support in place, especially if I have a history of trauma.
