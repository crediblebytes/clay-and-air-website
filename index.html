Breath library

The one system you can reach directly.

Your breathing runs itself all day and drags your nervous system along with it. Take it over for three minutes and the whole thing changes gear. 19 ways to do that, sorted by what is actually wrong.

Pick what is true right now

Relaxing

Foundational breath

The fastest way down. Base for everything else.

The drop comes on the exhale, not the inhale. By the third round your shoulders are lower than you left them and the noise in your head has thinned out.

AnxiousAngryBreathing shallow
  1. In through the nose: belly, chest, then up behind the eyes
  2. Hold 5 to 10 seconds at the top
  3. Open your mouth and sigh it all the way out
  4. Ujjayi exhale instead anywhere you cannot make noise
  5. Repeat

Relaxing

Box breathing

Quiets the mind. Used under pressure.

The count is the point. Five numbers is just enough to hold your attention, and while it is holding your attention it is not on the room. Things get quiet from the outside in.

Under pressureScatteredAngry
  1. Take one ordinary breath in through the nose and let it go out of the mouth
  2. Inhale 5 seconds through the nose: belly, chest, head
  3. Hold 5 seconds
  4. Exhale 5 seconds through the nose
  5. Hold 5 seconds
  6. Keep the count steady rather than long
3-10 minutes

Relaxing

Turtle breath

Deep relaxation and sleep.

It arrives in stages rather than all at once. Each step up the ladder takes a bit more of the day with it, and the last few rounds are the ones that put you out.

Can't sleep
  1. Run a few rounds of foundational breath first
  2. Lying down
  3. Release from the top down with an Ujjayi exhale
  4. Start at 10 seconds in, 10 seconds out
  5. Move to 12, then 15, then 18, then 20 as each one stays comfortable
  6. Stay at whatever count you can hold without effort
5-10+ minutes

Nadi Shodhana

Evens out both sides. Settles without sedating.

It settles without sedating. The static clears, and where that leaves you depends on when you run it. Clear enough to work in the afternoon, quiet enough for bed at night.

WiredScatteredCan't sleep
  1. Sit upright, spine long
  2. Close your right nostril with your thumb
  3. Inhale 7 seconds through the left: belly, chest, head
  4. Hold 5 seconds
  5. Exhale 7 seconds through the left
  6. Switch
3-10 minutes

Relaxing

Full-fill

Stacked breaths and a hold. Goes deeper than one round.

The stack does something a single breath does not. By the hold you are already further down than one round of foundational breath takes you, and the sigh finishes it.

Breathing shallowAnxiousCan't sleep
  1. Breathe in through the nose: belly, chest, head
  2. Second big breath, the same way
  3. Third big breath
  4. Hold 10 seconds at the top
  5. Sigh it out, or use a long Ujjayi exhale
3-10 minutes

Relaxing

4·3·6

Longer exhale than inhale. When box isn't enough.

Easy to keep going, which is the whole point. It is the one you can run for ten minutes without it turning into a job, and the long exhale does the rest.

AnxiousUnder pressure
  1. Sitting or lying down
  2. Inhale 4 seconds through the nose, deep into the belly
  3. Hold 3 seconds
  4. Exhale 6 seconds through the nose, slow
  5. Start again without pausing at the bottom
5-10+ minutes

Relaxing

Cleansing breath

Golden light in, smoke out. Puts the day down.

Slower to arrive than the counted techniques. The change tends to show up around the point the cauldron feels empty, and it keeps going after you stop.

AnxiousUnder pressureCan't sleepCarrying something
  1. Sitting or lying down
  2. In through the nose, a golden light coming in with the air
  3. Hold it at the top
  4. Ujjayi exhale, the tension leaving as dark purple smoke
  5. The heavy part settles in a cauldron low in the belly
  6. Breathe the light down into the cauldron and wash it clean
5-10 minutes

Relaxing

Qi Gong breathing

Standing, slow, with a travelling picture.

Steady rather than sedating. The stance is half of it, and you finish more awake than you started.

WiredScattered
  1. Stand with your feet planted and your knees soft
  2. In through the nose: belly, chest, head
  3. A warm gold light coming in with the air
  4. Ujjayi exhale, following it down to the tips of your toes
  5. Breathe in and bring it back to the top of your head
  6. Keep it circulating
1-5 minutes

Activating

Priming breath

Wakes the body up. Morning, or before training.

You feel it fast. Warm, awake, and buzzing slightly, the way you do after the first minute of a hard walk.

FlatBefore training
  1. Sit or stand with room to move your arms
  2. Take a couple of ordinary breaths first, in through the nose and out through the mouth
  3. Forceful inhale through the nose
  4. Forceful exhale through the nose
  5. Keep the arms and the breath on the same beat
  6. As many sets as you want
30 reps a set

Activating

Breath of fire

Forceful both ways. Builds heat.

Heat first, then your stomach starts to burn. Afterwards there is a lightness that takes a minute to settle.

FlatColdBefore training
  1. Sit upright
  2. Forceful exhale through the nose
  3. Forceful inhale through the nose
  4. Keep both halves equally strong and let them run together
  5. Start slow, then build the pace up
  6. One to five minutes
1-5 minutes

Activating

Buteyko breathing

A held walk. Runs while you are moving.

It pulls you out of your head and into your legs. By the second or third hold the chatter has stopped and you are just counting steps.

AnxiousUnder pressure
  1. Breathe in through the nose
  2. Exhale all the way out through the nose
  3. Pinch your nose closed and hold
  4. Walk
  5. Let go, take one good breath in, and let it go
  6. Repeat as you walk
15-25 steps a round

Activating

Kapalabhati

Forceful exhale only. Works the stomach.

Your stomach does the work and you feel it there first. Past five minutes it goes very quiet.

Flat
  1. Sit upright with one hand flat on your belly
  2. Take a deep breath in through the nose and let it go out of the mouth
  3. Forceful exhale through the nose
  4. Let the inhale happen by itself
  5. Keep going
  6. Fifty reps, or two to seven minutes
50 reps or 2-7 minutes

Activating

Igniter breath

Warms you up fast. After cold water, or before training.

Heat, fast. Three breaths in and you feel it in your face. As good for shifting a stuck head as a cold body.

FlatColdBefore training
  1. Sit up tall
  2. Take one foundational breath first: in through the nose, sigh it out
  3. Intense inhale through the nose
  4. Exhale through the mouth with the same force
  5. Keep both halves equally hard
  6. Thirty seconds to five minutes, or a hundred reps
30 seconds to 5 minutes

Activating

Twisting breath

Twist and exhale hard. Warm-up that works the core.

Your stomach is doing most of it. It also puts you somewhere your body does not usually go, which is half the point.

Before trainingFlat
  1. Arms out in front, palms together
  2. Twist to one side, breathing in gently through the mouth
  3. At the end, force it all out through the nose, stomach in hard
  4. Twist back the other way, breathing in across
  5. Force it out at the end of that side too
  6. Alternate for about ten minutes
About 10 minutes

Heart opening breath

Loud on the exhale. Clears the deck before the day.

Loud, and faintly ridiculous for the first minute. Then it stops being ridiculous and you notice how much you were holding on to.

Carrying somethingAngryFlat
  1. Sit or stand with your arms out wide
  2. In through the nose, arms drawing in toward the chest
  3. A green light coming into the heart space as they close
  4. Open the arms and breathe out on a long, audible ah
  5. Shake it out if something comes up
  6. Ten to twenty minutes

Kundalini Bhastrika

Breath of fire, arms overhead. Harder.

The arms are the hard part. Somewhere past a minute the burn in your shoulders takes over and the breathing starts running itself.

FlatBefore training
  1. Get breath of fire going on its own first
  2. Breathe in, then let about three quarters of the air back out
  3. Raise your arms overhead, elbows locked, thumbs pointing up and out
  4. Start the pump and keep the arms where they are
  5. Thirty seconds to a minute to begin with

Activating

Digestion igniter

Lion's breath and a stomach vacuum. Standing.

Odd, and unmistakable. You feel the vacuum up under your ribs rather than in the stomach muscles, and standing up on the inhale is the part that wakes you.

Flat
  1. Stand up
  2. Big breath in through the nose
  3. Lion's breath out: tongue out, Ujjayi, everything emptied
  4. Hands on your knees with your lungs empty
  5. Move it around while it is held there
  6. Stand and breathe in through the nose when you want air
10-15 minutes

Activating

Dynamic breathwork

Forty breaths and holds. The big one in this pillar.

Tingling in the hands and the face after the first round, then a wave of nervous anxiety on the empty hold. That wave is the whole point. Past it you come out light, warm and very awake.

FlatBefore training
  1. Forty controlled breaths through the mouth
  2. Breathe everything out and hold on empty
  3. Stay completely still to begin with
  4. Shake out and move when the stress response arrives
  5. Big breath in, sip a little more on top, let it go on an Om
  6. Finish with a relaxing technique and lie still

Freedom

Sonic Neural Breathwork

The deep one. A facilitated ceremony.

The first ten minutes are work and your head argues the whole way. Then it stops arguing and something else takes over. Most first times are sensory rather than emotional, tingling and cramping hands, which is the usual way in. Later ones can go somewhere else entirely.

  1. Flat on your back with something over your eyes
  2. Someone with you who understands what this is
  3. Set an intention
  4. In through the nose, out through the mouth: belly then chest
  5. Ninety percent in, ninety percent out
  6. Move and make noise. The track runs 55 minutes

The library

What these are

Breathing patterns you can run with nothing you do not already have. You have probably tried one of them badly under pressure and found it did nothing. Most of them take three minutes when you run them properly.

Every technique here is written so you can follow it alone, at the point you need it, without reading an essay first. Read one all the way through once while you are calm. Then it is there at 3am.

Where these come from

Ben Holt

Every technique on this site is one of his, learned through Awakened Breath and the 21 Day Breathwork Academy. The three pillars are his framework. The counts, the mechanics and the order they are taught in are his. Two of them are his own designs: Dynamic breathwork, built on the Wim Hof method, and Sonic Neural.

What is ours is the wording and the way it is arranged here, so you can find the right one quickly and follow it on your own. If you want the source, go to him: awakenedbreath.org.
